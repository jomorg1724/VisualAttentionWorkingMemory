05 · Architecture, from pixels to choice

A complete account
of the implemented observer.

The model contains 381,654 learned parameters in the completed 16-task continuation (381,264 in its 13-task parent). One spatial attention distribution selects fused visual evidence and local temporal comparisons. A spatial GRU and a recurrent CLS state carry information forward. Sixteen small output layers provide the task-specific response vocabularies.

This section specifies the actual implementation, including its constants and approximations. The mathematical descriptions follow the implementation; the neuroscience links explain the ideas motivating its computations. The model, loss, and renderer sources are recorded by hash in the accompanying provenance ledger.

How to read the notation

A subscript usually indexes time, location, or channel; a superscript such as “gray,” “pos,” or “selected” labels a kind of quantity and names that quantity's role. A hat denotes an estimate or prediction, a tilde denotes a transformed value, and a bar denotes a local average or explicitly defined effective quantity. Square brackets with a semicolon concatenate vectors. The symbol \(\odot\) means multiply corresponding entries. The symbol \(*\) denotes convolution, \(\|x\|_2\) is Euclidean vector length, and \(x^\mathsf T y\) is a dot product. Learned matrices are named \(W\), convolution kernels \(K\), and biases \(b\), with labels identifying their layers. The same letter in a different tutorial is defined locally; grayscale never uses the ambiguous letter G.

For each equation below, the adjacent explanation names its inputs, output, and operation. Numbers such as 64 and 100 are dimensions, while small constants such as \(10^{-8}\) prevent division by zero. Each symbol is defined alongside its equation.

Reference · Notation and causal state

Let \(X_t\in[0,1]^{3\times100\times100}\) be frame \(t\), with \(t=0,\ldots,T-1\). Let \(i\in\{1,\ldots,N\}\), \(N=100\), index a 10 × 10 spatial grid. Learned feature width is \(D=64\). We omit the batch index unless it is important. The recurrent states are a spatial field \(H_t\in\mathbb R^{64\times10\times10}\), a global CLS vector \(c_t\in\mathbb R^{64}\), a local motion-population trace \(Z_t\), and an attention-selected comparison trace \(m_t\in\mathbb R^{735}\). Spatial and CLS states and \(m\) start at zero on every trial; the local population trace starts uniform over displacements.

“CLS” is a convenient name for a recurrent summary vector. One CLS query produces a distribution over 100 spatial locations. The model has no task-conditioned query, task-specific sensory encoder, or task identity embedded in its input tokens. Task identity selects the output layer only. Fixed sensory windows require recent history; the implementation computes their causal equivalents from the supplied movie tensor before the recurrent loop, with no use of later frames in earlier-time features.

Reference · Appearance and simple local statistics

A convolution is a weighted sum over a local neighborhood. For output channel \(o\), input channel \(k\), output row and column \(r,c\), kernel row and column \(u,v\), stride \(s\), and padding \(p\), the library operation is \(Y_{o,r,c}=b_o+\sum_{k,u,v}K_{o,k,u,v}X_{k,sr+u-p,sc+v-p}\). Here \(Y\) is the output image of features, \(X\) is the input, \(K\) contains learned kernel weights, and \(b_o\) is the learned bias of output channel \(o\), with out-of-image entries zero. The same weights are used at every location. The library computes cross-correlation, applying the kernel in its stored orientation. Local selectivity and organized receptive fields provide a biological motivation, while exact weight sharing and backpropagation remain engineering assumptions. [[hubel1962]]

Center the RGB input by subtracting 0.5. A convolutional pathway applies a 5 × 5 convolution with stride 2 (3 → 16 channels), GELU, a 3 × 3 convolution with stride 2 (16 → 32), GELU, and adaptive average pooling to 10 × 10:

\[A_t=\operatorname{Pool}_{10\times10}\!\left[\operatorname{GELU}\!\left(K_2*\operatorname{GELU}\!\left(K_1*(X_t-0.5)+b_1\right)+b_2\right)\right].\]
Symbols and meaning. \(A_t\) is the 32-channel appearance field at frame \(t\). \(K_1,K_2\) and \(b_1,b_2\) are the two layers’ learned kernels and biases. The star applies each convolution over the image. GELU transforms each channel value nonlinearly; Pool averages the second layer to 10 × 10 sites. Read from the innermost parentheses outward: center pixels, convolve, transform, convolve, transform, pool.

The intermediate grids are 50 × 50 and 25 × 25. Adaptive pooling maps the latter to the common 10 × 10 grid, with overlapping input receptive fields. Each output site can depend on overlapping input neighborhoods. Convolution supplies spatially shared feature detectors, while the learned filters determine which appearance properties are useful. Retinotopically organized feature processing motivates this spatial layout.

For input size 25 and output size 10, adaptive pooling averages indices from \(\lfloor25i/10\rfloor\) up to, but excluding, \(\lceil25(i+1)/10\rceil\) along each axis. Adjacent bins can overlap. GELU applies \(\operatorname{GELU}(x)=x\Phi(x)\), where \(\Phi(x)=\frac12[1+\operatorname{erf}(x/\sqrt2)]\) is the standard-normal cumulative distribution. It smoothly attenuates negative values. Later, sigmoid \(\sigma(x)=1/(1+e^{-x})\) produces fractions between zero and one, and \(\tanh(x)=(e^x-e^{-x})/(e^x+e^{-x})\) produces bounded signed candidate states. These nonlinearities let the response depend on combinations of feature activation levels.

At each nonoverlapping 10 × 10 input block \(\Omega_i\), compute three centered channel means and a grayscale energy. Here grayscale is the arithmetic RGB mean \(I_t^{\rm gray}(x,y)=[X_{t,R}(x,y)+X_{t,G}(x,y)+X_{t,B}(x,y)]/3\). Here \(X_{t,R}\), \(X_{t,G}\), and \(X_{t,B}\) are the red, green, and blue pixel channels of the same frame; the capital letters after the comma name channels. The symbol \(I_t^{\rm gray}\) denotes the arithmetic average of the RGB channels:

\[\mu_{t,i,k}=\frac1{100}\sum_{p\in\Omega_i}(X_{t,k,p}-0.5),\quad e_{t,i}=\frac1{100}\sum_{p\in\Omega_i}(I^{\rm gray}_{t,p}-0.5)^2,\quad a^{\rm raw}_{t,i}=[\mu_R,\mu_G,\mu_B,\sqrt{e_{t,i}}].\]
Symbols and meaning. \(\Omega_i\) is the set of 100 pixels in image block \(i\); \(p\) indexes a pixel and \(k\) an RGB channel. \(\mu_{t,i,k}\) is that block’s mean channel value after subtracting 0.5. \(e_{t,i}\) averages squared grayscale deviation from 0.5; its square root is an intensity-scale feature. \(a^{\rm raw}_{t,i}\) concatenates four numbers. The shortened labels \(\mu_R,\mu_G,\mu_B\) refer to the same time and block. Example: an entirely 0.7-gray block has grayscale RMS 0.2, although its within-block standard deviation is zero.

The fourth number is RMS deviation from the fixed gray level. This distinction matters for a uniformly bright patch. RGB information survives both through these means and through the CNN even when the grayscale correspondence pathway discards chromatic distinctions. [[sensory-krauskopf1992]]

Reference · Fixed correspondence bank: 100 sites, three lags, 81 alternatives

Extract 24 × 24 grayscale neighborhoods at stride 10 after padding the centered image by seven zero-valued pixels on each side. These larger neighborhoods overlap while their centers align with the spatial grid. Subtract each patch's mean, then multiply by a separable, nonperiodic Hann window:

\[w(n)=\tfrac12\left[1-\cos\!\left(\frac{2\pi n}{23}\right)\right],\qquad P_{t,i}(x,y)=\left(Q_{t,i}(x,y)-\overline Q_{t,i}\right)w(x)w(y).\]
Symbols and meaning. \(n,x,y\) are integer positions from 0 to 23 within a crop. \(w(n)\) is a one-dimensional Hann weight, zero at the endpoints. \(Q_{t,i}\) is the extracted centered grayscale crop and \(\overline Q_{t,i}\) its scalar pixel mean. \(P_{t,i}\) is the mean-subtracted crop multiplied by the horizontal and vertical Hann weights. P denotes the prepared image patch.

The window reduces the influence of hard patch boundaries. The implementation fixes this receptive-field width at 24 pixels. For each nominal lag \(\ell\in\{1,2,4\}\), compare with frame \(t_\ell=\max(0,t-\ell)\). At startup, the earlier index repeats frame zero: there is no special mask suppressing these shortened-history comparisons.

\[F_{t,i}=\operatorname{FFT}_{32\times32}(P_{t,i}),\quad R_{t,i}^{\ell}=\operatorname{IFFT}\left(\overline{F_{t_\ell,i}}F_{t,i}\right),\quad E_{t,i}=\sum_{x,y}P_{t,i}(x,y)^2.\]
Symbols and meaning. \(F_{t,i}\) is the complex Fourier transform of the windowed patch after zero-padding to 32 × 32. FFT and IFFT are the forward and inverse transforms. A bar over the complex F means complex conjugation, unlike the scalar mean bar over Q. \(R^\ell_{t,i}\) is a grid of correlation scores between the earlier frame \(t_\ell\) and current frame t. \(E_{t,i}\) is total squared energy of the windowed crop; it differs from the 10 × 10 raw grayscale energy e above.

Zero padding to 32 × 32 permits efficient correlation. Only shifts in \(\{-4,\ldots,4\}^2\) are retained, using modular FFT indices for negative offsets. The bank retains the central ±4-pixel shifts needed for its candidate set. The retained small-shift region fits inside the zero-padding margin. With the chosen conjugation order, a rightward displacement of shared structure favors positive \(d_x\).

Why does multiplying Fourier transforms compute correlation?

For a zero-padded patch, the discrete transform is \(F(u,v)=\sum_{x,y=0}^{31}P(x,y)e^{-2\pi\mathrm{i}(ux+vy)/32}\). The inverse uses the opposite sign and divides by \(32^2\). A translation becomes a frequency-dependent phase rotation. Multiplying the later transform by the complex conjugate of the earlier one compares these phases; the inverse transform collects their agreement at each shift. Equivalently, expanding the sums and using orthogonality of the complex exponentials gives \(R(d_x,d_y)=\sum_{x,y}A(x,y)B(x+d_x,y+d_y)\), with circular indices on the padded grid. The zero margin prevents wraparound contamination for the retained small shifts. FFT evaluates this fixed transform efficiently.

\[C_{t,i,\mathbf d}^{\ell}=\frac{R_{t,i}^{\ell}(\mathbf d)}{\max(\sqrt{E_{t_\ell,i}E_{t,i}},10^{-8})},\qquad r_{t,i}^{\ell}=\frac{\sqrt{E_{t_\ell,i}E_{t,i}}}{\sqrt{E_{t_\ell,i}E_{t,i}}+0.01}.\]
Symbols and meaning. \(\mathbf d=(d_x,d_y)\) is a candidate displacement in pixels. \(C^\ell_{t,i,\mathbf d}\) divides correlation R at that displacement by the geometric mean of the two crop energies. The maximum imposes a numerical floor of \(10^{-8}\). \(r^\ell_{t,i}\) is a separate reliability factor between zero and one, using the constant 0.01. For negligible energy, r suppresses the contribution even if a normalized score would be misleading.

The reliability \(r\) is an energy-based attenuation factor. It measures prepared-image energy and can be large for strongly textured, ambiguous patterns. Longer lags make small per-frame motion easier to separate spatially but also increase sensitivity to dot replacement and displacements leaving the search range. Nominal velocity ranges shrink with lag: ±4, ±2, and ±1 pixels per frame per axis.

Reference · A small learned refinement and a distribution over shifts

Reshape each 81-value correlation vector into its 9 × 9 displacement lattice. A shared two-layer convolutional refinement operates over the 9 × 9 displacement grid: 1 → 4 channels with a padded 3 × 3 convolution, GELU, then 4 → 1 with another padded 3 × 3 convolution. Its 77 learned parameters are shared across sites, times, and lags. The final layer is initially zero, so the initial refined score equals the fixed correlation.

\[\widetilde C=C+0.1f_\theta(C),\quad \tau_\ell=\operatorname{clip}(e^{\eta_\ell},0.015,0.2),\quad p_{t,i,k}^{\ell}=\frac{\exp(\widetilde C_{t,i,k}^{\ell}/\tau_\ell)}{\sum_{j=1}^{81}\exp(\widetilde C_{t,i,j}^{\ell}/\tau_\ell)}.\]
Symbols and meaning. \(f_\theta\) is the small learned convolutional refinement, with parameters collectively denoted \(\theta\). \(\widetilde C\) is the refined score. \(\eta_\ell\) is a learned log-temperature; exponentiation makes temperature positive and clip restricts it to the stated interval. \(p^\ell_{t,i,k}\) is the normalized score for candidate k, and j runs over all 81 candidates in the denominator. Scores sum to one separately at each time, site, and lag. Here p denotes a displacement weight.

Temperatures initialize at 0.04. Lower temperature concentrates the weights around the highest scores. The hard clamp also means that the raw log-temperature receives zero clamp derivative outside its active range. Full population codes can represent uncertainty or competing hypotheses more richly than a single best displacement, and here the normalized scores represent displacement-matching weights. [[simoncelli1998]] [[ma2006]]

\[\widehat{\mathbf d}_{t,i}^{\ell}=\sum_{k=1}^{81}p_{t,i,k}^{\ell}\mathbf d_k,\qquad \widehat{\mathbf v}_{t,i}^{\ell}=\widehat{\mathbf d}_{t,i}^{\ell}/\ell.\]
Symbols and meaning. \(\mathbf d_k\) is the fixed two-dimensional displacement of candidate k. The hat marks the weighted displacement estimate \(\widehat{\mathbf d}\). Dividing it by nominal frame lag \(\ell\) gives velocity \(\widehat{\mathbf v}\) in pixels per observation. This expectation averages all candidate displacements using their weights.

The divisor remains the nominal lag during startup, even when the clamped frame index makes the actual separation smaller. The resulting transient is part of the implementation. No angle, target location, event boundary, or correct flow vector is supplied to train this stage.

Reference · Predict the next image from the preceding flow estimate

Average the three lag-specific velocities equally to obtain a 10 × 10 flow grid. Bilinearly upsample it to 100 × 100 without multiplying its pixel units. The prediction of frame \(t\) uses the flow already available at \(t-1\):

\[\bar{\mathbf v}_{t-1,i}=\tfrac13\sum_{\ell\in\{1,2,4\}}\widehat{\mathbf v}_{t-1,i}^{\ell},\qquad \widehat I^{\rm gray}_t(\mathbf x)=I^{\rm gray}_{t-1}\!\left(\mathbf x-\operatorname{Upsample}(\bar{\mathbf v}_{t-1})(\mathbf x)\right).\]
Symbols and meaning. \(\bar{\mathbf v}_{t-1,i}\) averages the three lag-specific velocities available at the preceding frame. \(\mathbf x\) is a destination pixel coordinate. Upsample interpolates the coarse field to that pixel. \(\widehat I_t^{\rm gray}\) is the predicted grayscale image; it samples the actual preceding image \(I_{t-1}^{\rm gray}\) at the displayed source coordinate. If the prior estimate is two pixels rightward, a destination pixel reads from two pixels to its left.

This is backward sampling: each destination pixel asks where its source should have been. It extrapolates constant velocity using the previous-image estimate on the destination grid. The image sampler is bilinear, uses border padding, and uses endpoint-aligned normalized image coordinates. The flow upsampler uses non-endpoint-aligned bilinear interpolation. Differentiable resampling connects the pixel error to the learned correspondence refinement. [[jaderberg2015]]

How does bilinear sampling predict a fractional pixel?

Suppose the requested source is \((x_0+a,y_0+b)\), with integer \(x_0,y_0\) and fractions \(a,b\in[0,1]\). The prediction is \((1-a)(1-b)I^{\rm gray}_{x_0,y_0}+a(1-b)I^{\rm gray}_{x_0+1,y_0}+(1-a)bI^{\rm gray}_{x_0,y_0+1}+abI^{\rm gray}_{x_0+1,y_0+1}\). The four weights sum to one. A source halfway between two horizontally adjacent pixels therefore uses equal weights on them. Border padding clamps out-of-image coordinates to the edge. The resulting image is differentiable almost everywhere with respect to displacement, allowing an image-prediction error to adjust the learned refinement without a labeled velocity target.

\[\epsilon_{t,i}=\frac{\frac1{100}\sum_{p\in\Omega_i}(\widehat I^{\rm gray}_{t,p}-I^{\rm gray}_{t,p})^2}{\max\{(e_{t-1,i}+e_{t,i})/2,10^{-4}\}},\qquad \omega_{t,i}=\frac{\sqrt{e_{t-1,i}e_{t,i}}}{\sqrt{e_{t-1,i}e_{t,i}}+10^{-4}}.\]
Symbols and meaning. \(\epsilon_{t,i}\) is the block’s mean squared prediction error divided by its average raw grayscale energy across adjacent frames. \(\omega_{t,i}\) is a separate energy-based weight for the auxiliary loss. The numerator compares predicted and observed grayscale at the same destination pixel. Both occurrences of \(10^{-4}\) are fixed stabilizing constants. Here epsilon denotes the normalized prediction error.

The error \(\epsilon\) enters the visual features; \(\omega\epsilon\) contributes to the auxiliary loss. At the first frame the error feature is zero. At the second frame the previous velocity came from startup self-correlations. A newly appearing photograph or changed illumination can produce a large error without meaningful physical motion. Predictive-processing theories motivate sensitivity to mismatch. Here, a scalar mismatch feature and an auxiliary photometric objective implement that comparison. [[rao1999]] [[friston2005]]

Reference · Compare local histories before selecting a location

Two distinct memory mechanisms process the motion population. First, a learned-rate local trace supplies a short adaptive reference. With \(\rho=\sigma(\zeta)\), initialized at 0.1,

\[\delta_{t,i}^{\ell}=\sum_k|p_{t,i,k}^{\ell}-Z_{t-1,i,k}^{\ell}|,\qquad Z_{t,i,k}^{\ell}=Z_{t-1,i,k}^{\ell}+\rho r_{t,i}^{\ell}(p_{t,i,k}^{\ell}-Z_{t-1,i,k}^{\ell}),\quad Z_{-1,i,k}^{\ell}=1/81.\]
Symbols and meaning. \(Z^\ell_{t-1,i,k}\) is the preceding local population trace; \(p^\ell_{t,i,k}\) is the current population. \(\delta\) sums their absolute differences over candidates. \(\rho=\sigma(\zeta)\) is one learned rate shared by the trace, where \(\zeta\) is an unconstrained scalar. Multiplication by reliability r slows replacement when the crop has little energy. The new trace moves partway toward p; its initial 81 entries are each 1/81.

The difference is computed before updating the trace. Weak image energy slows trace replacement. This leaky population memory has one learned write rate.

Second, explicit causal windows compare two local histories. At time \(t\), reference samples occupy \(t-15\) through \(t-8\); recent samples occupy \(t-3\) through \(t\). Four observations lie between the windows. Negative indices are absent, and incomplete startup windows retain their full nominal widths when computing reliability. For \(W\in\{R,O\}\), with nominal width \(L_R=8\), \(L_O=4\),

\[\bar p_{W,t,i,k}^{\ell}=\frac{\sum_{s\in W_t,\,s\ge0}r_{s,i}^{\ell}p_{s,i,k}^{\ell}}{\max\{\sum_{s\in W_t,\,s\ge0}r_{s,i}^{\ell},10^{-6}\}},\qquad q_{W,t,i}^{\ell}=\frac1{L_W}\sum_{s\in W_t,\,s\ge0}r_{s,i}^{\ell}.\]
Symbols and meaning. W identifies either the reference window R or the recent window O. \(W_t\) is the set of frame indices belonging to that window, s indexes its available frames, and \(L_W\) is its nominal length. \(\bar p_W\) is the reliability-weighted mean population. \(q_W\) is total available reliability divided by nominal length, so an incomplete window has reduced confidence. If no reference frames exist, both its population and confidence are zero.

Define \(q=\sqrt{q_Rq_O}\), \(\Delta p=|\bar p_O-\bar p_R|\). For each lag, concatenate three scaled 81-dimensional populations and two scalar distances:

\[b_{t,i}^{\ell}=\left[10q\bar p_R,\;10q\bar p_O,\;10q\Delta p,\;q\sum_k\Delta p_k,\;q\left(1-\frac{\bar p_R^\mathsf T\bar p_O}{\max(\|\bar p_R\|,10^{-8})\max(\|\bar p_O\|,10^{-8})}\right)\right].\]
Symbols and meaning. \(q=\sqrt{q_Rq_O}\) requires confidence in both windows. \(\Delta p\) is their entrywise absolute difference, distinct from the feature width D=64. The brackets concatenate three 81-entry vectors and two scalars. The last scalar uses cosine similarity: a dot product divided by vector lengths. \(b^\ell_{t,i}\) therefore contains 245 entries, and all three lags give 735. In a seven-frame VDA trial the reference window never becomes available: q and this entire comparison vector stay zero.

Concatenating the three lags yields \(b_{t,i}\in\mathbb R^{735}\), since \(3(3\cdot81+2)=735\). The implementation orders all three reference populations, then all three recent populations and differences, followed by the six scalars; this ordering fixes the coordinates used by the saved checkpoints. The factor 10 rescales population features and remains in this model. It is distinct from the old, removed 100-fold amplification of attention-weighted recurrent input.

Comparing at each location preserves which patch changed. If populations were pooled first, a target change could be canceled or mimicked by a distractor. Attention selects the relevant comparison, which the classifier uses to form the response. At short trial lengths the reference confidence is zero; the model then relies on its other pathway.

Reference · Assemble the visual token

At each site, concatenate 32 appearance features, four raw statistics, 243 reliability-scaled population values, three reliabilities, three differences from the adaptive trace, six velocity components, and one prediction error:

\[f_{t,i}=\left[A_{t,i},a^{\rm raw}_{t,i},\{10r_{t,i}^{\ell}p_{t,i}^{\ell}\}_{\ell},\{r_{t,i}^{\ell}\}_{\ell},\{\delta_{t,i}^{\ell}\}_{\ell},\{\widehat{\mathbf v}_{t,i}^{\ell}\}_{\ell},\epsilon_{t,i}\right]\in\mathbb R^{292},\quad V_{t,i}=W_f f_{t,i}+b_f+e_i^{\rm pos}.\]
Symbols and meaning. \(f_{t,i}\) collects the named local features into one 292-entry vector; braces mean concatenate the entries for all three lags. \(V_{t,i}\) is the resulting 64-entry visual token. \(W_f\) maps 292 inputs to 64 outputs, \(b_f\) is its learned bias, and \(e_i^{\rm pos}\) is a learned 64-entry position code. This position code is distinct from the windowed image patch P and the grayscale energy e.

Here \(W_f\in\mathbb R^{64\times292}\), and \(e_i^{\rm pos}\) is a learned positional vector initialized with standard deviation 0.02. Position makes location distinguishable even when two patches look alike. The 735-channel comparison is kept separate until attention pooling; it retains its 735 coordinates alongside the visual tokens. Multiple sensory representations meet before the selector, while a wider temporal comparison route is selected by that same map.
