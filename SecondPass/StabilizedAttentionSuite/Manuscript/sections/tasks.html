The battery contains thirteen computational assays adapted from the cited research. Keeping the implemented stimuli fixed makes architectural comparisons interpretable. The distinctions below explain each task's information requirements and experimental design.

What the variants change

Image recognition offers 0, 4, 12, or 24 study images, followed by three blank frames and 3, 4, or 5 repetitions of the same probe. N0 is an empty-list control; H counts repetitions of the same probe image. Cued orientation, motion-duration, and binding tasks offer 0, 4, 12, or 24 blank delay frames. Krauzlis B12/B20/B28 varies baseline motion duration. The selectors navigate the exported native conditions.

Task-by-task rationale

Why this combination is more demanding than motion alone

Cardinal motion asks whether correspondence can be extracted. Cued motion change asks whether locally extracted evidence can be selected using a vanished cue. Motion-duration reporting asks whether multiple transitions can be integrated and retained. These are distinct computations: a system that identifies the final motion direction may fail a duration judgment, and a detector of “any change anywhere” fails foil trials in the cued task. Motion-selective neurons and motion discrimination provide a biological foundation for studying these distinctions, but the particular raster sizes and schedules are engineering choices. [[britten1992]] [[spatial-roitman-2002]] [[spatial-arcizet-2018]]

The appearance tasks require color, form, and image-identity information. Chromatic discrimination requires an informative color representation. Contour discrimination requires relationships between orientations and locations; its shuffled control preserves the orientation inventory. Spatial binding requires those relationships to survive a delay until a retrospective spatial query. Recognition asks whether the current image belongs to a preceding list of distinct photographs. It tests exact image membership in the presented list. [[sensory-krauskopf1992]] [[sensory-field1993]] [[spatial-griffin-2003]] [[spatial-brady-2008]]

A demanding control for the common-map hypothesis

For the two-frame sensory tasks, the long-window comparison branch has no usable reference history and contributes zero. Success must therefore be supported by the appearance/motion features and recurrent CLS pathway. The architecture is allowed to use different representations; the test concerns a shared spatial selection mechanism.

Units, labels, and evaluation

Delays are expressed in observation counts; the Krauzlis recipe additionally specifies a 100-Hz presentation rate. Each repetition of the probe contributes another recurrent update using the same pixels. The atlas displays the native glyphs present during blank intervals. A label of 1 has a task-specific meaning: it may identify the second interval, a positive axial rotation, a cued-direction change, a spatial exchange, or a previously seen photograph. Pooling these labels into a universal “change” class would change the experiment.

Four-choice direction and duration tasks have chance balanced accuracy of 1/4; other nondegenerate tasks have chance 1/2. Results should be reported per task and per condition before an aggregate is shown. Empty-list recognition has only negative trials and is evaluated with specificity and false-positive rate. Photo-based training and evaluation use disjoint BSDS500 identity partitions. New stream seeds create fresh draws from the same underlying image database. [[arbelaez2011]]
