THE FINDINGS

Attention matters. The two benefits are not yet recovered.

Both motion and static selection are vulnerable.

In Krauzlis, at the trained 26°/28° changes, balanced accuracy fell from 85.6% to 33.5% with target inhibition (−8), and to 24.7% with distractor stimulation (+8). In signed orientation, target inhibition and distractor stimulation also impaired performance. This supports a causal role for the shared map in both tasks in this checkpoint.

Neither planned benefit is established.

All eight primary tests failed to establish an accuracy benefit after correction. Near threshold, Krauzlis target stimulation gave +0.35 or +0.87 percentage points, with intervals spanning zero; distractor inhibition changed no choices. Orientation distractor inhibition gave approximately zero change, while target stimulation reduced accuracy by 16.2 or 20.7 points. More target attention is not automatically better.

The delay result deserves a separate experiment.

In the independent psychometric set, signed orientation at 15° scored 50.8% with no blank delay, versus 87.5% with four blank frames. The conditional curves distinguish missed aligned rotations from incorrect reports for opposite rotations. Extra recurrent processing is a possible explanation; these data do not yet isolate its cause.

There is little distractor attention left to remove.

In the selected Krauzlis condition (baseline 20, 18° target changes, 32 scenes), control post-change attention averaged 96.7% at the target and 0.06% at the distractor. This is consistent with limited room for distractor inhibition to help. Strong logit biases can also disrupt an already learned allocation. Neither explanation should be treated as a proven mechanism without a follow-up test.

Psychometric curves reveal graded sensitivity below the original task strengths. Recognition also worsens with larger study loads and favors more recent images. The complete measurements, uncertainty, controls and attention movies follow below.

02 / MATCHED CAUSAL TESTS

Can attention help—and hurt?

Identical pixels, different priority-map biases. We test target stimulation and distractor inhibition for benefits, and the reverse manipulations for costs. The perturbation acts inside the recurrent model; its consequences unfold through later memory and decisions.

“Stimulation” here means adding a number to the model’s attention logits. “Inhibition” means subtracting it. These experiments do not reproduce electrical microstimulation or biological silencing.

04 / REPRODUCIBILITY & LIMITS

What these experiments establish.

Frozen model, fresh trials

The validation-selected suite checkpoint at update 54,808 is evaluated without an optimizer or any weight changes. New trial namespaces are separate from training, validation, and the earlier final test. Natural-image trials use the held-out photograph pool; new trials are not new photo identities.

Faithful task rendering

At native parameter values, the isolated strength adapters produce bit-identical pixels to the original generators. Smaller evidence magnitudes are deliberately extended tests. Zero magnitude has no physical direction change; its scheduled class is not a true positive event.

Matched comparisons

Each intervention and its control share exactly the same input movie. Spatial, dose and timing controls act before softmax. Adding the same logit to all locations is a normalization control and should change neither the attention distribution nor the choice.

Uncertainty and replication

Paired intervals resample scene clusters, preserving repeated angles and events, stratified by condition, target and sign (also class for cued orientation). When every observed paired difference is zero, a zero-width bootstrap interval would overstate certainty: we instead bound the effect by ±[1 − 0.05^(1/n)], using the finite sample of n scene clusters. Eight primary benefit tests receive Holm correction. The remaining screen is exploratory. This is one trained checkpoint, not independent training-seed replication.

Exact attention projection and averaging

For query q and spatial patch j, the requested projection is aj = (1/Q) Σq Aqj. Here Q = 1. The 100 weights reshape to 10 × 10 and each patch becomes a 10 × 10 pixel block. No interpolation or per-frame rescaling changes the apparent location. Color is log(1 + 100a)/log(101). Overlays combine 58% input image and 42% map color. Saved arrays retain the original floating-point model inputs and weights; display images are quantized to 8-bit.

Means are calculated within a native task condition and a spatially meaningful group. For cued memory, location is fixed to the top-left; signed orientation also separates cue signs. Krauzlis separates left/right target and target/foil/catch events. Recognition separates seen/novel probes at each load and hold. Averaging photographs produces a blurred mean image, so the default shows an individual photo and its own weights, with the condition mean in a separate panel.

Weights describe the model’s routing of information. A bright patch is not automatically a causal explanation; the intervention experiment is the separate causal test.

Intervention geometry and interpretation

Target and distractor masks use patch centers within 12 pixels of the aperture or Gabor center. In orientation, “distractor” is one fixed next-index uncued Gabor, not all three distractors and not chosen according to its rotation. “Both” is the union of those two sites. Off-site is centered at (50,20). The cue-glyph arm is a radius-9 mask centered at the glyph’s upper-edge location, (target x, target y − 23); it samples the upper cue region rather than the entire glyph.

Krauzlis cue = frames 0–1; baseline = reference frame 7 through the frame before the first changed image; postchange = changed images through the final moving frame; report = final fixation image. Orientation cue-only = frame 0; sample = frames 1–2 (glyph also visible); delay = native blank frames; probe = final image. A zero-length delay makes the delay-only arm a no-op by construction.

Krauzlis balanced accuracy uses target hit rate and native-weighted negatives: ½[hit + 1 − (29 × foil FA + 14 × catch FA)/43]. Cued-orientation “yes” means the target rotated in the cue’s sign; its negatives mix opposite and unchanged targets. Binary signal-detection summaries are descriptive and cannot uniquely distinguish biological sensitivity from selection or decision bias.

  • Lovejoy & Krauzlis (2010), Inactivation of primate superior colliculus impairs covert selection of signals for perceptual judgments — motivates spatially selective suppression and distractor competition tests.
  • Sridharan et al. (2017), Does the superior colliculus control perceptual sensitivity or choice bias during attention? — motivates separating accuracy, hits, false alarms and criterion.
